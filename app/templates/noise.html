How to Interpret

  • σ = 0 (none) — the clean baseline. Perfect data means perfect recovery; this is the upper bound on achievable accuracy.
  • σ = 0.5 (medium) — matches the actual TCP server's noise level. This row represents real-world expected performance.
  • R² stays high even at σ = 1.0 because linear regression averages out Gaussian noise effectively with 300 samples.
  • MAE / RMSE grow with noise — the model's coefficient estimates become noisier, so test-set predictions shift slightly.
  • Optimization agreement drops at high noise because noisy coefficients shift the LP solution. If the match score falls below 0.95, the learned optimizer is drifting from the true optimum.
  • Constraint safety can fail at extreme noise if the learned purity coefficient is distorted enough that the LP finds a different feasible vertex.
